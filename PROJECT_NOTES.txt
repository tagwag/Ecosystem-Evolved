=== MODEL BASICS ===
- World: 1920x1080, patchy food, cells smell only concentration at their own spot (no direction).
- Body rules from the Java Cell.java (size slows you, food = 100 energy, size +10 per food).
- Worm brain: real C. elegans connectome (Cook 2019, OpenWorm c302): 300 neurons + 95 muscles,
  4,562 chemical synapses, graded rate model. Genome = one strength gene per synapse + gains + decoder.
- Fair test = one genome copied to 40 worms on unseen worlds, same layouts for every condition.

=== RESULTS SO FAR (short) ===
Step 1 tiny evolved brain: 1.7x random walk.
Step 2 worm connectome: 4.4x random walk. Evolution mostly prunes. Brain is highly redundant
  (no single neuron/synapse matters; mutational robustness). Evolved wiring survives food scarcity, random doesn't.
Step 3 hunger peptide (broadcast signal, evolvable receptor per neuron): no effect.
  Open readout (decoder reads all 300 neurons): evolution skips command neurons AVA/AVB, reads smell
  sensors + first relays (ASE, AWC, AWA, AIY, AIZ). Weakens the old "rediscovered AVB" claim.
Step 3b hunger + scarce food (4 seeds): weak hint.  Step 3c (12 seeds): NOT confirmed (p=0.64 energy,
  p=0.33 slowing-when-full; direction right 8/12 vs 4/12). Cause: resting saves too little energy.
Step 3d (32 seeds, GPU, 2026-11-02): satiety slowing CONFIRMED but small: full/hungry speed 0.98 vs 1.05
  (p=0.0004; below 1 in 19/32 vs 8/32). But hunger brains hold LESS energy: 3.84 vs 4.08 (p=0.006; still
  p=0.01 without outlier seed 27) and eat less (9.0 vs 9.8). Peptide makes worms rest when full, and resting
  doesn't pay in this world -> costs fitness. Same lesson: make resting worth it (higher move cost / danger).
Step 3e (move_cost sweep 0.25/0.5/1/2, 32 seeds): 0.25 = 3d exactly (deterministic). 0.5: no difference (p=0.65).
  1.0 and 2.0: hunger brains "win" big on energy (1.77 vs 1.05; 2.02 vs 0.43, p<0.0001) BUT eat almost nothing
  (food 1.17 vs 4.75; 0.03 vs 0.94). REWARD HACKING: fitness = average energy held, and sitting still while
  slowly starving (600 energy over ~1000 ticks -> avg ~2.0) beats moving when moving is too expensive. The
  peptide gives a steady internal signal brains use to shut movement off. NOT evidence the peptide helps foraging.
  Fix before rerun: fitness that punishes starving (energy at end, or longer generations so idlers die early).
Step 3e2 (same sweep, fitness = energy left at end + survival): 0.25: no fitness difference (p=0.30). 0.5: peptide
  slightly better (+0.24, p=0.036; not significant after correcting for 4 tests) while eating LESS (8.0 vs 8.7) =
  a hint of real efficiency. 1.0/2.0: unlivable worlds (moving costs more than food gives); peptide brains idle to
  survive longest (food ~1 and 0.03), still a degenerate win. Takeaway: the peptide works as an "off switch" for
  movement; it only helps where resting truly pays. The 2x/4x/8x costs were an arbitrary sweep, not biology.
  Better next: a physical reason to rest (predators that notice moving prey; digestion while still).
Step 4g (growth, 32 seeds, GPU): growth NEUTRAL confirmed: food 18.63 vs 18.63 (p=0.99). Brains grew 1-10 neurons (drift).
Step 3f (ASH starvation alarm x peptide, 32 seeds, end fitness): NULL. Alarm p=0.65/0.55, peptide p=0.30, no interaction.
Step 8 (6 predators, 100 gens): predators cost a lot (fitness 1.75 vs 2.63, p<0.0001) but TOUCH DOES NOT HELP
  (bites 1.08 vs 1.05, p=0.37): prey never evolved escape. ODD: fitness peaks ~gen 20 then slowly declines in every
  arm, even without predators -> investigate (noisy single-world selection? harmful mutations piling up in elites?).
Step 8b (predators notice motion): peptide again slows worms when full (0.98 vs 1.05, p=0.0001) and eats less, but
  fitness gain not significant (+0.15, p=0.17), bites not reduced, no interaction with motion-sensing (p=0.58).
Step 8c (8b + digestion while still): peptide HURTS (fitness 0.97 vs 1.53, p=0.0001). Bimodal: ~2/3 of peptide seeds
  FREEZE (0 food, fitness ~0.62 = survive on start energy) = stuck in a local optimum; the rest forage like no-peptide.
BIG PATTERN Steps 3d-8c: the peptide reliably becomes an "off switch" for movement; evolution often gets trapped
  by it (freeze/idle) and it never turns into a clear fitness gain. Next ideas: escape from the freeze trap (start
  energy lower / longer generations so freezing can't survive), and fix the late-generation fitness decline first.
Step 4 brain growth (duplicate neuron / new neuron / new synapse, cap 50): brains grew 3-6 neurons by
  DRIFT; growth selected at chance rate = neutral. Lesson: new parts need a new job in the world.
Step 5 lifetime learning ladder (two-odor world, odor meaning flips per world): fixed -> hebb -> dopamine ->
  curiosity -> sleep, + sprint_rest. After the learning-rule fix: fixed 18.8, hebb 17.7, dopamine 12.0, curiosity
  13.8, sleep 12.4, sprint_rest 13.7 (random 5.7). NO step helps. Evolution turned learning rates down. Cause: fixed
  brain eats equally in both odor meanings -> task never needed learning. Retest needs a COSTLY decoy. Code worm5/step5.
Step 6 curriculum (explore 20 gens, then puzzle spots) x evolvable memory pool, 4 seeds x 60 gens:
  curriculum -> more puzzle food (1.0-1.3 vs 0.4-0.6 per worm, 7/8 pairs) but p=0.08 = HINT, not confirmed.
  Puzzle-only brains ~random (0.53). Memory: no effect (p=0.51), pool drifted 4 -> ~6, cost total food (9.8 vs 12.2).
  Sequence puzzle ~never solved. Puzzle food (~1) tiny vs normal food (~12) -> weak selection.
Step 6b (12 seeds x 100 gens): curriculum hint GONE (p=0.98) = was noise. Memory no help, leans harmful
  (p=0.07). Seed luck huge (0.15-3.35 puzzle food). Results in results/step6b_*.
Step 7 randomness check (seeded PCG64 x6 vs OS entropy x6, 40 gens): NO difference (all p >= 0.15). No RNG bug.
BIG PATTERN Steps 3-6: every added brain feature is null because the world doesn't demand it. Make the WORLD
  harder next (costly decoy, bigger puzzle payoff, predator) before adding more brain features.
Step 13 (2026-11-03, GPU, clonal-group scoring, 60 clones x 6 fixed worlds; results/step13{a,b,c}_stats.json):
  old nulls redone. Clonal scoring did NOT rescue them.
  13a (Step 8, 32 seeds x 100 gens): touch vs no touch fitness 2.73 vs 2.59 (+0.14, p=0.12, 21/32), bites 1.10 vs
    1.19 (p=0.11) -> leans helpful, not significant. Predators still cost -1.15 (p<0.0001, 0/32).
  13b (Step 3f, 32 seeds x 60 gens, 2x2): alarm with peptide 0.00 (p=0.99); alarm without peptide +0.20 (p=0.068,
    hint). Peptide without alarm -0.05 (p=0.63); peptide WITH alarm -0.25 (p=0.0085, 10/32). Best arm: no peptide +
    alarm (4.07). Fits "hunger overrides aversion" (peptide blunts the alarm), but one test with 4 comparisons.
  13c (Step 4, 16 seeds x 60 gens): growth food 19.00 vs 18.91 (+0.09, p=0.23); grown 1-8 neurons. Still neutral.

Step 14a peptide baggage (2026-11-04, 32 seeds, Step 12a brains -> scarce food + predators, 100 gens): NOT baggage.
  Brains DEPEND on their peptide: knocking it out costs -0.70 in their own world (3.88 vs 3.18, p=0.0075) and drops
  skill 2.65 -> 2.03 in the new world. But knocked-out brains recover within 10 gens and end level or ahead:
  knock 2.86, frozen 2.86, nopep 2.80, keep 2.75 (keep vs knock p=0.20, vs frozen p=0.31). Keep gained least
  (+0.10, p=0.33) vs frozen +0.21 (p=0.005), nopep +0.25 (p=0.008): hint that evolving receptors slows adaptation
  (extra mutations), not significant. Verdict: entrenched (wired around) but cheap to remove = no "stopped evolution".
Step 14b selfish-herd predators (pred_loner=1, pred_confuse=1, 32 seeds x 150 gens): WEAK herding. Herd arm's
  nearest-worm distance fell 99.9 -> 92.6 (p=0.002), but plain predators also fell 102 -> 96; herd vs plain at the end
  -3.5 (p=0.051). Fitness herd 3.03 vs plain 2.81 (p=0.016) partly because the herd world is easier. Bites equal.
  No scent vs scent: clumping same (p=0.46) but food lower (6.24 vs 6.84, p=0.007) -> scent may help find food via
  others (social foraging), not herding. Effect sizes small (~4%).
Step 15 lineage tournament (b14_knock, 12 founders x 5, 32 tournaments x 300 gens): picked by 'biggest gain',
  which was a FLAWED rule: b14_knock's gain is recovery from the knockout, not more evolution. Takeover is fast:
  one lineage owns 100% after median 7 gens (all 32 fixed). Winners: seed 26 x20, 21 x10, 6 x1, 19 x1 (4 of 12).
  Seed 26 was the best founder on retest (3.50); founder ranking by one test is noisy (+-0.3), retest skill vs wins
  rho=0.23 p=0.48. Champions 2.99 (gen 50) -> 3.18 (gen 200-300, plateau), below the best founder (-0.32, p<0.001)
  and level with their own founder (-0.11, p=0.26). Lesson: lineages fix almost at once; the world's skill ceiling
  (~3.2-3.5) caps everyone; one test per brain isn't enough to rank brains.

Step 16 (2026-11-07, 16 peaks re-ranked on 18 worlds, each evolved twice, 150 gens, tested on 12 fresh worlds):
  NO world broke the plateau. Base-world skill FELL in every arm, control included: w_base 3.35 -> 3.10 (p<0.001),
  w_seasons 2.90, w_fastpred 3.00, w_digest 2.82 (all vs start p<0.001). New worlds were also worse than the
  control (seasons -0.20 p=0.006, digest -0.28 p=0.001, fastpred -0.10 p=0.23). Own-world skill also fell in
  seasons (-0.15) and fastpred (-0.24); only digest rose (1.20 -> 2.55: they started unadapted).
  Likely cause: TRAINING/TEST MISMATCH. Evolution scores brains in 1/6-size clonal worlds (10 worms, 1 patch,
  1 predator, 1 world per generation = noisy); the plateau peaks were picked in full-size worlds, so evolution
  re-fits them to the small world and they lose full-world skill. The plateau may partly be this mismatch.
Step 17 (Step 15 champions, same 4 worlds, 100 gens): no recovery. w_base flat (3.23 -> 3.23); new worlds lower
  it (seasons -0.11, fastpred -0.17 p=0.04, digest -0.28 p=0.004). All arms below the best founder (3.44,
  p<0.001); 3-8 of 32 runs beat it. fastpred own-world skill +0.24 (p=0.001) but it didn't carry over.
Step 18 (herd predators + obstacles, 32 seeds x 150 gens): NO hiding. Time near rock flat at ~8% (= random
  start, p=0.73); bites slightly UP over evolution (+0.06, p=0.056); time near rock doesn't predict bites
  (rho=-0.15, p=0.41). Rock made life worse: fitness -0.55, food -1.36 vs no rock (both p<0.001). Herd vs plain
  predators with rock: no differences. Likely limits: the only rock sense is bumping (nose touch), worms can't
  sense predators before contact, and noisy small-world scoring.

Step 18b (2026-11-08, re-test of Step 18 brains, all worms incl. dead): rock = neither shelter nor predator trap.
  Bite rate near rock = open (1.48 vs 1.48 per 1000 worm-ticks, p=0.99). Deaths 25-45% higher near rock
  (p=0.008 herd, 0.0007 plain) -> probably weak/starving worms ending up stuck there (no food grows in rock).
  Hidden (in range, sight blocked) ~4%, stuck ~2%, unchanged by evolution.
Step 19 (2026-11-08, matched scoring: 60 clones in full-size worlds, 2 worlds/brain/gen, 16 seeds x 16 brains,
  300 gens; yardstick = 12 fixed unseen worlds): skill KEEPS CLIMBING, slowly, in both arms (no flat plateau yet).
  base: 2.54 (g20) -> 3.33 (g300), peak at the last checkpoint; g200 vs g100 +0.21 (p=0.002), g300 vs g200 +0.10
    (p=0.11); slope over the last 100 gens +0.13 per 100 gens, 11/16 seeds rising. From random brains it reached the
    old 'plateau' level (~3.3) and is still going -> matched scoring removed the decline seen in Step 16.
  rich (all senses): 1.76 -> 2.18 (lower: harder world with costs), +0.12 per 100 gens, 12/16 rising, g300 vs g200
    +0.08 (p=0.13). Did NOT climb faster or longer than base within 300 gens; both are decelerating.
Test bench brain_dials (16 Step 19 base brains, toxin world they never trained in, 18 combos x 12 worlds, 277 s on GPU):
  command-layer gain and the serotonin-like modulator both scale the command layer, so they interact strongly;
  best skill at ~half the normal command input (gain 0.8 x serotonin level 1); full gain 1.5 or modulator 2 is worst.
  ASH knockout: no clear effect. Fits explain 39-70% of variation.

Progress-chasing teacher (2026-11-09, 8 seeds x 16 brains x 300 gens per arm, 4.3 h): the teacher LOST.
  Fixed (base world only) beat it on intelligence (holdout worlds) 2.94 vs 2.46 (p=0.008, 0/8 seeds) and on pool
  skill 2.20 vs 1.85 (p=0.015). Teacher vs random rotation: no difference (p=0.6). Teacher and random both DECLINED
  over time (teacher 2.55 -> 2.46, random 2.76 -> 2.39 intelligence); fixed stayed ~2.9-3.0 (flat).
  Teacher's picks: toxin 10 blocks, fast predators 9, base 5, temperature 3, moving food 2, scarce 1.
  Likely flaw: its 'progress' signal mostly measured recovery from its own world switches (score drops on arrival,
  rebounds within the block), so it chased the worlds that hurt most. Switching every 10 gens also caused
  forgetting. Fixes to try: measure progress on a fixed check (not within-block rebound), longer blocks, or train
  on a mix of worlds every generation instead of switching.

=== KNOWN ISSUES / FIXES ===
- 2026-11-01 FIXED: food-tie bias. world.eat() let agents eat in list order and evolution puts elites first,
  so parents won food ties. Now random order each tick (own RNG, layouts unchanged). Steps 1-4 + 3b/3c were
  run with the bias: their FAIR TESTS are unaffected (identical genomes), but evolution itself was slightly
  tilted toward parents. Steps 5 and 6 were restarted with the fix.
- 2026-11-01 FIXED: learning could only strengthen. Rates are >= 0 and rewards were >= 0, so Hebbian and
  dopamine updates were always positive: in a dopamine brain 87% of plastic synapses sat at the cap and neurons
  fired at 0.84 (saturated). First Step 5 run: fixed 18.8 food vs every learner ~5-9 (random walk 5.7), all
  learners got WORSE during life. Fix: covariance Hebb (rate minus each neuron's running mean) + dopamine as
  reward prediction error (reward minus running expected reward). Same genome after fix: 0% at cap, ~50% of
  weights negative, food 3.1 -> 9.7. Flawed runs kept in runs/old_ratchet, results/old_ratchet. Steps 5+6 rerun.
  step5/step6 now skip finished runs (resume after a time limit).
