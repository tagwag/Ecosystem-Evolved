=== MODEL BASICS ===
- World: 1920x1080, patchy food, cells smell only concentration at their own spot (no direction).
- Body rules from the Java Cell.java (size slows you, food = 100 energy, size +10 per food).
- Worm brain: real C. elegans connectome (Cook 2019, OpenWorm c302): 300 neurons + 95 muscles,
  4,562 chemical synapses, graded rate model. Genome = one strength gene per synapse + gains + decoder.
- Fair test = one genome copied to 40 worms on unseen worlds, same layouts for every condition.

=== RESULTS SO FAR (short) ===
Step 1 tiny evolved brain: 1.7x random walk.
Step 2 worm connectome: 4.4x random walk. Evolution mostly prunes. Brain is highly redundant
  (no single neuron/synapse matters; mutational robustness). Evolved wiring survives food scarcity, random doesn't.
Step 3 hunger peptide (broadcast signal, evolvable receptor per neuron): no effect.
  Open readout (decoder reads all 300 neurons): evolution skips command neurons AVA/AVB, reads smell
  sensors + first relays (ASE, AWC, AWA, AIY, AIZ). Weakens the old "rediscovered AVB" claim.
Step 3b hunger + scarce food (4 seeds): weak hint.  Step 3c (12 seeds): NOT confirmed (p=0.64 energy,
  p=0.33 slowing-when-full; direction right 8/12 vs 4/12). Cause: resting saves too little energy.
Step 3d (32 seeds, GPU, 2026-11-02): satiety slowing CONFIRMED but small: full/hungry speed 0.98 vs 1.05
  (p=0.0004; below 1 in 19/32 vs 8/32). But hunger brains hold LESS energy: 3.84 vs 4.08 (p=0.006; still
  p=0.01 without outlier seed 27) and eat less (9.0 vs 9.8). Peptide makes worms rest when full, and resting
  doesn't pay in this world -> costs fitness. Same lesson: make resting worth it (higher move cost / danger).
Step 3e (move_cost sweep 0.25/0.5/1/2, 32 seeds): 0.25 = 3d exactly (deterministic). 0.5: no difference (p=0.65).
  1.0 and 2.0: hunger brains "win" big on energy (1.77 vs 1.05; 2.02 vs 0.43, p<0.0001) BUT eat almost nothing
  (food 1.17 vs 4.75; 0.03 vs 0.94). REWARD HACKING: fitness = average energy held, and sitting still while
  slowly starving (600 energy over ~1000 ticks -> avg ~2.0) beats moving when moving is too expensive. The
  peptide gives a steady internal signal brains use to shut movement off. NOT evidence the peptide helps foraging.
  Fix before rerun: fitness that punishes starving (energy at end, or longer generations so idlers die early).
Step 3e2 (same sweep, fitness = energy left at end + survival): 0.25: no fitness difference (p=0.30). 0.5: peptide
  slightly better (+0.24, p=0.036; not significant after correcting for 4 tests) while eating LESS (8.0 vs 8.7) =
  a hint of real efficiency. 1.0/2.0: unlivable worlds (moving costs more than food gives); peptide brains idle to
  survive longest (food ~1 and 0.03), still a degenerate win. Takeaway: the peptide works as an "off switch" for
  movement; it only helps where resting truly pays. The 2x/4x/8x costs were an arbitrary sweep, not biology.
  Better next: a physical reason to rest (predators that notice moving prey; digestion while still).
Step 4 brain growth (duplicate neuron / new neuron / new synapse, cap 50): brains grew 3-6 neurons by
  DRIFT; growth selected at chance rate = neutral. Lesson: new parts need a new job in the world.
Step 5 lifetime learning ladder (two-odor world, odor meaning flips per world): fixed -> hebb -> dopamine ->
  curiosity -> sleep, + sprint_rest. After the learning-rule fix: fixed 18.8, hebb 17.7, dopamine 12.0, curiosity
  13.8, sleep 12.4, sprint_rest 13.7 (random 5.7). NO step helps. Evolution turned learning rates down. Cause: fixed
  brain eats equally in both odor meanings -> task never needed learning. Retest needs a COSTLY decoy. Code worm5/step5.
Step 6 curriculum (explore 20 gens, then puzzle spots) x evolvable memory pool, 4 seeds x 60 gens:
  curriculum -> more puzzle food (1.0-1.3 vs 0.4-0.6 per worm, 7/8 pairs) but p=0.08 = HINT, not confirmed.
  Puzzle-only brains ~random (0.53). Memory: no effect (p=0.51), pool drifted 4 -> ~6, cost total food (9.8 vs 12.2).
  Sequence puzzle ~never solved. Puzzle food (~1) tiny vs normal food (~12) -> weak selection.
Step 6b (12 seeds x 100 gens): curriculum hint GONE (p=0.98) = was noise. Memory no help, leans harmful
  (p=0.07). Seed luck huge (0.15-3.35 puzzle food). Results in results/step6b_*.
Step 7 randomness check (seeded PCG64 x6 vs OS entropy x6, 40 gens): NO difference (all p >= 0.15). No RNG bug.
BIG PATTERN Steps 3-6: every added brain feature is null because the world doesn't demand it. Make the WORLD
  harder next (costly decoy, bigger puzzle payoff, predator) before adding more brain features.

=== KNOWN ISSUES / FIXES ===
- 2026-11-01 FIXED: food-tie bias. world.eat() let agents eat in list order and evolution puts elites first,
  so parents won food ties. Now random order each tick (own RNG, layouts unchanged). Steps 1-4 + 3b/3c were
  run with the bias: their FAIR TESTS are unaffected (identical genomes), but evolution itself was slightly
  tilted toward parents. Steps 5 and 6 were restarted with the fix.
- 2026-11-01 FIXED: learning could only strengthen. Rates are >= 0 and rewards were >= 0, so Hebbian and
  dopamine updates were always positive: in a dopamine brain 87% of plastic synapses sat at the cap and neurons
  fired at 0.84 (saturated). First Step 5 run: fixed 18.8 food vs every learner ~5-9 (random walk 5.7), all
  learners got WORSE during life. Fix: covariance Hebb (rate minus each neuron's running mean) + dopamine as
  reward prediction error (reward minus running expected reward). Same genome after fix: 0% at cap, ~50% of
  weights negative, food 3.1 -> 9.7. Flawed runs kept in runs/old_ratchet, results/old_ratchet. Steps 5+6 rerun.
  step5/step6 now skip finished runs (resume after a time limit).
